Q: Nov25 Q12, Nov25 Q21a, Apr26 Q11, Apr26 Q24a, Apr24 Q12
<p>A <b>missing value</b> is stored as <code>NaN</code> ("not a number") or <code>None</code>. Find them with <code>df.info()</code>, <code>df.isnull()</code> and <code>df.isnull().sum()</code>.</p>
<h4>Why they matter</h4>
<ul><li>They bias statistics (mean, correlation) and can give a wrongly biased model.</li><li>Most machine learning algorithms cannot take NaN.</li><li>Dropping rows or columns blindly loses valuable information.</li></ul>
<h4>Why they occur</h4>
<p>Data corruption, incomplete data entry, human error, users deliberately skipping sensitive questions, survey non-response.</p>
<h4>Handling</h4>
<table class="work"><tr><th>Method</th><th>Command</th><th>When</th></tr>
<tr><td>Drop rows</td><td><code>df.dropna()</code> / <code>dropna(how='any', subset=['col'])</code></td><td>few values missing</td></tr>
<tr><td>Drop columns</td><td><code>df.dropna(axis=1)</code></td><td>column mostly empty</td></tr>
<tr><td>Constant</td><td><code>df.fillna(0)</code></td><td>rarely right: 0 may look like data</td></tr>
<tr><td>Mean</td><td><code>df['c'].fillna(df['c'].mean())</code></td><td>symmetric numeric data</td></tr>
<tr><td>Median</td><td><code>fillna(df['c'].median())</code></td><td>skewed numeric data</td></tr>
<tr><td>Mode</td><td><code>fillna(df['c'].mode()[0])</code></td><td>categorical data</td></tr>
<tr><td>Group mean</td><td><code>groupby('g')['c'].transform('mean')</code></td><td>same class as the tuple</td></tr>
<tr><td>Previous / next</td><td><code>ffill()</code> / <code>bfill()</code> (<code>fillna(method='ffill')</code>)</td><td>ordered data</td></tr>
<tr><td>Interpolation</td><td><code>interpolate(method='linear')</code></td><td>sequence or time series</td></tr>
<tr><td>Model</td><td><code>SimpleImputer</code>, <code>KNNImputer(n_neighbors=3)</code>, regression/logistic regression</td><td>most probable value</td></tr></table>
<p>Other cleaning steps: remove duplicates, fix data types, fix inconsistent spellings, treat outliers, normalise.</p>

<h4>Exploratory data analysis (EDA)</h4>
<p>EDA is looking at the data before modelling: understand columns and types, clean it, describe it with statistics, study one variable (histogram, box plot), then relationships (scatter plot, correlation), detect outliers, and decide on features and the model. Visualisation shows distribution, relationships and outliers at a glance and communicates results to non-technical readers.</p>
