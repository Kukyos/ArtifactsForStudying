=== Apr24 Q13
<p><b>Correlation analysis</b> measures how strongly two variables are related and in which direction. The coefficient lies between -1 and +1: +1 perfect positive, -1 perfect negative, 0 no linear relationship. It shows association, not cause and effect.</p>
<ul><li><b>Pearson</b>: linear relationship between continuous variables, r = sum (x - xbar)(y - ybar) / sqrt(sum (x - xbar)^2 x sum (y - ybar)^2); sensitive to outliers.</li><li><b>Spearman</b>: monotonic relationship using ranks, rho = 1 - 6 sum d^2 / (n (n^2 - 1)); used for non-linear, ordinal or ranked data.</li></ul>
<p>Covariance shows the direction only (its size depends on units); correlation is covariance scaled by both standard deviations. Visualise with a scatter plot or a correlation heat map.</p>

=== Apr24 Q18
<p><b>Exploratory data analysis (EDA)</b> is the first look at a dataset, before modelling, using statistics and plots to understand it. Goals: understand the structure and quality of the data, find patterns and relationships, spot missing values and outliers, test assumptions, and decide how to clean the data and which model to use.</p>
<h4>Steps of EDA</h4>
<ol class="steps">
<li><b>Understand the data:</b> size (<code>shape</code>), column names, data types, a few rows (<code>head()</code>), and the meaning of each variable (numerical or categorical, target or feature).</li>
<li><b>Clean the data:</b> count and treat missing values (<code>isnull().sum()</code>, fill with mean, median, mode or drop), remove duplicates, fix data types and inconsistent entries.</li>
<li><b>Descriptive statistics:</b> mean, median, mode, standard deviation, quartiles for numerical columns (<code>describe()</code>); counts for categorical columns (<code>value_counts()</code>).</li>
<li><b>Univariate analysis:</b> study each variable alone: histogram for the distribution and skew, box plot for the spread and outliers, bar chart for categories.</li>
<li><b>Bivariate and multivariate analysis:</b> scatter plots, correlation (Pearson, Spearman) and a heat map, group-wise comparisons (<code>groupby</code>).</li>
<li><b>Detect outliers:</b> box-plot rule (below Q1 - 1.5 IQR or above Q3 + 1.5 IQR) or z-scores; decide to keep, cap or remove them.</li>
<li><b>Conclusions:</b> which features matter, whether to transform or scale, and which technique suits the problem.</li>
</ol>

=== Apr24 Q23a
<p><b>Content-based filtering</b> recommends items similar to those the user liked before ("show me more of what I have liked"). It uses the features of the items and a profile of the user, and does not need other users.</p>
<ol class="steps"><li><b>Item profile:</b> describe each item (genre, artist, keywords); text is converted to TF-IDF vectors.</li><li><b>User profile:</b> built from the items the user rated high or bought.</li><li><b>Similarity:</b> cosine similarity, sim(a, b) = sum(ai bi) / (|a| |b|), between the profile and each item.</li><li><b>Recommend</b> the most similar items the user has not seen.</li></ol>
<p>Example: a music app recommends more songs by the artist the user played most. Advantages: no other users needed, works for new items, easy to explain. Limitations: only similar items (over-specialisation), needs good item features, the new user needs a profile.</p>

<ol class="steps">

<li><b>Evaluation:</b> confusion matrix with accuracy, precision, recall and F1 on the test tweets.</li>
<li><b>Deployment and communication:</b> classify new tweets as they arrive, and show a dashboard of the share of positive and negative tweets over time, with the top complaints.</li>
</ol>

<p>A <b>recommender system</b> is a tool, an application of machine learning, that predicts what a user will like and suggests it automatically, from the user's preferences, past behaviour or the behaviour of similar users. Examples: Netflix and YouTube (movies), Amazon and Flipkart (products), Facebook and LinkedIn.</p>
<table class="work"><tr><th></th><th>Search engine</th><th>Recommender system</th></tr><tr><td>Type</td><td>pull: the user asks</td><td>push: the system suggests</td></tr><tr><td>Based on</td><td>keywords of the query</td><td>past preferences</td></tr><tr><td>Purpose</td><td>information retrieval</td><td>information discovery</td></tr></table>
<h4>Why recommender systems are needed</h4>
<ul><li>Too much information: users cannot browse millions of items.</li><li>Less effort for the user, and new items are revealed.</li><li>More sales, engagement and customer retention for the business.</li></ul>
<h4>Data used</h4>
<p><b>Explicit</b> (ratings, comments, likes) and <b>implicit</b> (searches, purchase history, clicks), plus information about the items (keywords, categories) and the users (profile), and the user-item interactions.</p>
<h4>Classification</h4>
<table class="work"><tr><th>Type</th><th>Idea</th><th>Method</th><th>Weakness</th></tr>
<tr><td><b>Content-based filtering</b></td><td>"show me more of what I liked": items similar to those liked before</td><td>item features, user profile, TF-IDF, cosine similarity</td><td>suggests only similar items</td></tr>
<tr><td><b>Collaborative filtering</b></td><td>"people like me liked this": the ratings of similar users (user-based) or similar items (item-based)</td><td>utility matrix, cosine or Pearson similarity, weighted average of the neighbours' ratings</td><td>cold start, sparse matrix</td></tr>
<tr><td><b>Hybrid</b></td><td>combines both</td><td>weighted, switching, mixed, cascade, feature combination, feature augmentation, meta-level</td><td>more complex</td></tr></table>
<p><b>Example of collaborative filtering:</b> user X rated Item 1 = 5 and Item 2 = 3; users Y and Z also rated Item 3. The cosine similarities of X with Y (0.767) and Z (0.471) weight their ratings of Item 3 into a prediction of 2.76 for X (see Nov24 Q24b).</p>
<p><b>Evaluation:</b> MAE and RMSE for ratings; hit rate and ARHR for top-N lists; coverage, diversity and novelty.</p>
